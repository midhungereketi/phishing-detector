# Dataset attribution and model provenance

Application source is covered by the repository's MIT license. Dataset terms are separate. Raw corpora are downloaded to the ignored `backend/data/` directory; they are not redistributed in this repository. Trained models are derived artifacts, not synthetic or hardcoded predictions.

## URL model

- **Dataset:** PhiUSIIL Phishing URL (Website), UCI Machine Learning Repository, 2024.
- **Creators:** Arvind Prasad and Shalini Chandra.
- **Source:** https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/967/phiusiil+phishing+url+dataset
- **Citation:** Prasad, A. & Chandra (2024). PhiUSIIL Phishing URL (Website). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cose.2023.103545
- **License:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), with attribution and no warranty or endorsement.
- **Modification:** Select 15,000 legitimate and 15,000 phishing URLs with seed 42, remove normalized duplicates, reject malformed URLs, discard all supplied webpage features and recompute 21 lexical features. UCI label `0` is phishing and `1` legitimate; the training pipeline reverses these so internal `1` always means phishing.
- Supplement with 10,081 unique eligible PhreshPhish publisher-training URLs (see below), for 40,081 total rows. No labels are generated by heuristics.
- Split by registered domain using the bundled Public Suffix List, including private hosting suffixes. Related subdomains share a group; train, tuning, calibration and test domains have zero overlap. Approximate 60/10/10/20 group proportions can produce different row proportions.
- Compare Random Forest and scaled Logistic Regression on tuning F1. Refit selected model on train+tuning, fit sigmoid calibration on separate calibration domains, and evaluate the untouched test split. Vectorizers are fitted without calibration/test data. Brier scores, reliability bins and threshold-specific errors are measured, not assumed to improve.

## Modern URL supplement and reserved test

- **Dataset:** [PhreshPhish v1.0.1](https://huggingface.co/datasets/phreshphish/phreshphish), publisher revision `eabec4b7a66324b79cc8a0ad856d1731dc26fe1a`.
- **Authors:** Thomas Dalton, Hemanth Gowda, Girish Rao, Sachin Pargi, Alireza Hadj Khodabakhshi, Joseph Rombs, Stephan Jou and Manish Marwah.
- **Paper:** [PhreshPhish: A Real-World, High-Quality, Large-Scale Phishing Website Dataset and Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10854), 2025, revised 2026.
- **License:** CC BY 4.0; publisher restricts intended use to anti-phishing research. Authors and dataset are not endorsing this project.
- **Modification:** Read URL, label and date columns only; discard HTML. Normalize `phish=1`, `benign=0`. Consider first 15,000 publisher-order rows from training shards `train-000..002`; exclude registered domains in diagnostic `test-000` and reserved final `test-001` slices, reject invalid URLs and deduplicate against UCI. 10,081 supplemental URLs remain after deduplication.
- The original UCI-only model achieved 41.77% accuracy / 93.92% false-positive rate on 924 eligible diagnostic rows, motivating modern supplementation. That diagnostic set informed development and is not a final untouched benchmark. The original diagnostic artifact is retained for audit in `backend/models/baseline_external_evaluation.json`.
- The final model was evaluated on the previously unexamined first 1,000 rows from `test-001`: 91 corpus-domain overlaps and 2 invalid URLs were excluded, leaving 907 rows collected September–December 2025. Accuracy 80.60%, precision 77.69%, recall 78.09%, F1 77.89%, false-positive rate 17.45% at threshold 0.5. No final-test threshold tuning or fitting occurred.
- This is a small publisher-order slice, not a representative random sample or the complete PhreshPhish benchmark. Supplemental training and final test share a publisher: this is a separate modern domain-disjoint test, not a completely independent-source benchmark. Labels remain publisher claims rather than our own audits. Raw slices/provenance are ignored; exported metrics contain dataset/model checksums.

## Email model

- **Phishing source:** José Nazario's [Phishing Corpus](https://monkey.org/~jose/phishing/), specifically `phishing-2023`.
- **License:** [CC BY 4.0](https://monkey.org/~jose/phishing/LICENSE.txt), per the author's [README](https://monkey.org/~jose/phishing/README.txt). Attribution: José Nazario; no endorsement or warranty. The author notes personal-inbox coverage, possible hand-label errors, and incomplete anonymization in recent mailboxes.
- **Legitimate source:** [Apache SpamAssassin public corpus](https://spamassassin.apache.org/old/publiccorpus/), `20030228_easy_ham` and `20030228_hard_ham`. We use only ham; spam is not relabeled as phishing.
- **Terms:** Copyright in SpamAssassin message text remains with the original senders; see the [corpus README](https://spamassassin.apache.org/old/publiccorpus/readme.html). Raw messages are not redistributed or exposed by the app.
- **Modification:** MIME parse, convert HTML to inert text, ignore attachments, normalize addresses/URLs/digits, deduplicate normalized text. Group matching normalized-text prefixes (first 120 characters) to reduce simple campaign leakage; this does not identify every campaign. Approximate 60/20/20 fit/calibration/test split with zero prefix-group overlap. TF-IDF unigrams/bigrams plus eight metadata features are fitted on training messages only; sigmoid calibration uses separate messages. Class weighting accounts for imbalance. Independent email JSONL evaluation is supported but no modern independent email dataset has been evaluated yet.

## Important interpretation limits

These are educational baselines. High held-out results are not proof of equivalent performance on real-world or current traffic. URL source-specific formatting can be predictive. Email ham (2003) and phishing (2023) differ greatly in age/source: the classifier may learn corpus differences rather than general phishing behavior. Exact normalization deduplication does not eliminate related campaigns or all near duplicates. An independent modern, mixed-source test set and campaign/time-separated evaluation are needed before operational use.

Model estimates are sigmoid-calibrated on held-out corpus data and scaled to 0–100. Calibration changed URL test Brier score from 0.05941 to 0.06188 (slightly worse) and email Brier from 0.00608 to 0.00573 (better). Calibration is not guaranteed to transfer to operational prevalence or unseen sources. Combined policy takes the maximum of model estimates and explicit threat floors; personal blocks force 100. It is not a probability and is not evaluated by individual-model matrices. Pasted Authentication-Results may be forged and is not independently verified.

Local ML, email analysis and feed matching do not contact targets. Opt-in URL checks resolve public DNS, inspect verified TLS and HEAD redirects, and query RDAP. Optional VirusTotal lookup discloses the URL only when selected; no rescan is submitted. Target page bodies, remote email images and attachment contents are never executed or retrieved. The extension adds ordinary-click checks and exact-host browser blocks with explicit limits; inbox filtering remains future work.

## Threat intelligence is not training data

[PhishTank](https://phishtank.org/developer_info.php) verified-online URLs are cached as exact-URL hashes, with feed import time and provider ID. Respect provider terms and download limits; application keys improve access. Raw feed entries are not distributed as model-training labels. A fresh match sets policy floor 95; stale (over 24 hours) floor 70. An absent URL never proves legitimate. Optional VirusTotal reports remain a separate evidence layer and must respect its API terms; results are cached for one hour. Malicious reports within seven days set explicit floors (one/two engines: 70; three or more: 90). These policy values are engineering choices, not learned probabilities or scientifically validated operating points.

Every downloaded corpus has a SHA-256 checksum recorded in `backend/models/metrics.json`; it also records seed, package version, sample counts, split method, algorithm comparison and test metrics. To reproduce, use `backend/requirements-lock.txt` with Python 3.12 and run the training command in README.md.
